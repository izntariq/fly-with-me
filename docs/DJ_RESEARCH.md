# DJ research

How 29 well-known DJs actually play live, and what each finding became in the app. The research was done on 28
September 2026 from interviews, gear breakdowns, set reviews and technique guides. Every URL used is listed at the
end.

**How much to trust it.** Public sources rarely give exact numbers like bar counts, effect settings or tempos. Two
tags mark weaker findings:
- **(convention):** a genre convention, not something documented about that DJ.
- **(uncertain):** couldn't be confirmed.

The styles in the app (`web/js/djstyles.js`) are interpretations of these findings, not recordings of anyone's set.

**What a style does.** The fly's neurons still decide *when* a move happens and *how intense* it is. A style decides
*which* moves each neuron group reaches for, which transitions and builds it prefers, how long its blends are, how
long a track stays on, its home tempo, and what it hypes the crowd with.

## Mainstage, big room and bass

| DJ | What the research found | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| **David Guetta** | Hybrid rig with CDJ-3000s, a DJS sampler and a TB-303. Plays house acapellas over his own re-made drums and chords. "Yo-yo" energy: a tender section, then a sudden hard drop. Mic hype before the first drop. Almost everything is his own edit. 126–130 BPM (convention). | **Live 303 acid line** in the track's key (the fly writes the pattern from its spikes). Mashups. Sing-along drop-out then slam. Snare-roll builds with horn. Lots of drop swaps and double drops. |
| **Martin Garrix** | Unreleased IDs plus his own anthems, mashups and live edits. Build (convention): 8–16 bars of accelerating snare roll, pitch riser, HPF and white noise, 1-beat gap, then a dry, punchy drop against a reverb-drenched breakdown. | **8-bar snare-roll builds** (the most frequent of any style), stutters, crush, CO2 blasts, double drops. |
| **Tiësto** | Modern sets: each track plays about 1.5–2 minutes ("a little bite from everything"): house, drum and bass, trance, techno. Older trance sets had long 10-minute tracks. | Tracks stay on 60–120 s, lots of cuts and drop swaps, snare builds. |
| **Calvin Harris** | "I'm a selector, not a DJ." Ableton-synced, plays his own hits in versions made for the set, tight phrase-perfect transitions, drums out for the crowd to sing. | Hard cuts on the phrase, sing-along drop-outs, 4-bar blends. |
| **Swedish House Mafia** | Four CDJs: the middle two for music, the outer two for acapellas and FX. "Building-block" transitions: a hi-hat loop, then drums from the other deck. Reverb only on the mids. SP16 drum rolls in builds. Crowd-sung finales. | **Mids-only reverb** (now the default for everyone), loop layering, mashups, snare fills, 8-bar snare builds, sing-alongs, CO2. |
| **DJ Snake** | A genre arc from hip-hop and R&B through house to trap. His 2014 Essential Mix had 47 tracks in 2 hours (~2.5 min each). Home-made mashups, e.g. a "Thriller" tease before trap drums take over. | Hard cuts, stutter builds, mashups, bass teases, horn and siren. |
| **Skrillex** | Genre whiplash (dubstep, garage, drum and bass, trap), 70–170 BPM. Early live sets: Ableton plus trigger pads, intro to outro, then a hard cut on the drop. Bold cuts rather than blends; effects as flourishes. | Hard cuts almost always, stutter edits, reverses, stutter builds, lasers. Tracks stay on 30–90 s. |
| **Diplo** | Serato, set lists played by mood. Dancehall, moombahton, baile funk, pop-rap and bass house in one set. Bootleg lineage; confetti and CO2 on the drops. | Cuts, mashups, bass teases, pumps, horn / CO2 / siren hype. |
| **Steve Aoki** | Fast electro, big room, trap and hardstyle cuts; mashups; cake and champagne crowd stunts. | Cuts, drop swaps, double drops, and a big "cake moment" impact (CO2 + horn + boom). |
| **Armin van Buuren** | Trance journeys at 132–140 BPM. 32–64-bar blends with EQ bass swaps and minimal effects. Loops and reverb to smooth transitions. Long breakdowns, then the kick "blows up" back in. | **32-bar blends**, filter builds, reverb, very few moves. |
| **Alok** | Brazilian bass: deep, filtered, punchy basslines with tight sidechain pumping. Classic songs taken somewhere new. | **Sidechain pump**, mashups, snare builds. |
| **Nucleya** | Desi bass: dhol and tumbi over trap and dubstep with Bollywood samples. The dhol drops out for bare electronic percussion and comes back "with twice the intensity". Bollywood sing-alongs. | Bass-out teases, stutter builds, dhol rolls, horn and siren. |

## Live, house and techno

| DJ | What the research found | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| **Fred again..** | Plays rather than just DJs: launches loops and clips in parallel and plays vocal slices, drums and melodies from pads (Maschine, Push 3), rebuilding tracks live. Vocal chops rearranged rhythmically and pitched up, into reverb and delay. On "Marea" the music drops almost to silence and the crowd carries the melody. USB sets are fast, genre-hopping b2bs. | **Live chops:** four 1-beat slices of the playing track, band-passed to the vocal range, retriggered on 16ths and pitched (0, +3, −5 semitones). The **fly's spikes write the chop pattern**. Sing-along drop-outs. Filter builds, no horns. |
| **Carl Cox** | Three-deck mixing, quick cuts landing exactly on the phrase. Cue-button builds. Subtle roll effects. RMX-1000 live sampling. Traktor Beat Masher and Flux loops (the track resumes where it would have been). Spinbacks. | **Cue-roll builds** (the original build here), loop layering from the other deck, spinbacks, slip-mode rolls. |
| **Richie Hawtin** | Additive mixing: loops layered across four decks, moved through tracks in 16/32-beat steps. Model 1 mixer: hybrid filters and a master resonant filter. A foot pedal for delay throws, "a third hand". | **Loop layering**, **Model 1 resonant filter sweep**, **dub delay throw**, 16-bar blends, rare filter builds. |
| **Charlotte de Witte** | Dark acid techno, 135+ BPM. Mixes in key, smooth transitions. Acid lines under filter sweeps, trans-gated pads (live use uncertain). | Trans gate, crush, resonant filter, acid line, 12-bar blends. |
| **Amelie Lens** | Fast techno, 136–148 BPM, old and new records. RMX-1000 sampler layers at the peak. Reads the crowd for 20+ minutes before playing. | Fast rolls, trans, pump, layers and chops, cue-roll builds. |
| **Peggy Gou** | Selector first: disco, house, techno and electro across a wide tempo range (90–150 BPM on DJ-Kicks). | Filter dips, echo and reverb, cuts that allow tempo jumps. |
| **Black Coffee** | Afro house at ~100–122 BPM. Mostly EQ mixing with heavy filters, patient long blends, restrained drops. Blends where the percussion interlocks (call and response). | **32-bar blends**, no cuts, no drop swaps, rare filter builds, the calmest style. |
| **Solomun** | Long narrative sets; reverb and delay on the outgoing track during transitions (source uncertain). | Long blends plus echo outs, reverb and flanger. |
| **Four Tet** | Very eclectic (UK garage, techno, ambient, his own edits); opens with ambience; fast, frantic swaps in b2b. | Chops, dub throws, layers, cuts. |
| **Jamie xx** | Crate-digger; loops and flips found records; UK garage and dubstep nods; Space Echo tape delay (live use uncertain). | Chops, dub throws, baby scratches, echo outs. |
| **Eric Prydz** | Extremely long blends where one lead runs across three tracks. The "Pryda snare": a compressed, sustained snare at the end of a bar. | **Pryda snare fill**, 32-bar blends, huge 8-bar snare builds. |
| **Fisher** | Tech house ~124 BPM. Plays full tracks, mixes over the outro. **Volume drop 1 bar before the new phrase**, then "hit the intro hard". A big track exactly 16 bars after the last drop. | **1-bar kill transition** (his favourite), bass teases, pump, filter builds. |
| **John Summit** | Vocal-led tech house, 124–128 BPM (technique sources thin; uncertain). | EQ and filter mixing, vocal mashups, pump, occasional kill. |
| **Kaytranada** | "Beat tape" sets: a new groove about every 30 s with deliberate contrast (house into R&B, hip-hop into funk). Tracks run 80–138 BPM with a Dilla-style swing. | Tracks stay on **25–60 s**, cuts, chops, filter dips. |

## Turntablists

| DJ | What the research found | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| **DJ Qbert** | Invented the **crab** scratch: 3–4 fingers tap the crossfader pinkie to index, thumb springs it back, so 4 rapid clicks per gesture. Credited with the clover tear. His crew (DJ Disk) invented the **orbit**, or 2-click flare. | **Crab** and **orbit** scratches added; flares and transformers everywhere. |
| **A-Trak** | DMC world champion at 15; scratching as "a means to an end" inside party sets; electro and Baltimore club swaps. | Chirps, transformers and flares inside fast cuts, snare builds. |
| **DJ Jazzy Jeff** | Popularized the **transformer** scratch (rhythmic crossfader flicks, like a tremolo); beat juggling; backspins. | Transformers, backspins and spinback cuts. |

## Moves added from this research

| Move | From | How it works here |
|---|---|---|
| Live chops | Fred again.. | 4 one-beat slices of the last bar, band-passed around 1.1 kHz, retriggered on 16ths with pitch shifts; the track ducks underneath |
| Sing-along drop-out | Fred again.. ("Marea"), Calvin Harris, SHM | Master low-pass to 220 Hz and gain to 30% for 4 bars, riser, then boom and slam back |
| Live 303 acid | David Guetta | Saw through a resonant (Q 14) low-pass with a per-note envelope; notes from the key; pattern from spikes |
| 1-bar kill | Fisher | Mix silenced for the last bar of the phrase (reverb tail rings), then cut to the other deck with a boom |
| Snare-roll build | Garrix, Guetta, Tiësto (convention) | Snare roll accelerating 1/4 → 1/8 → 1/16 → 1/32, rising in pitch and level, over the HPF sweep and riser |
| Stutter build / edit | Skrillex (convention) | Hard 1/8 → 1/32 rolls with bit-crush and a steep pitch rise; 1/32 roll plus gate |
| Filter build | house / techno | Long HPF sweep and reverb wash, no roll, no horn; the groove never stops |
| Double drop | big room (convention) | Both decks jump to their drop cue on the same beat with one bassline, then settle on the new deck after 16 bars |
| Mashup | Guetta, SHM, Diplo, DJ Snake | The other deck's mids (bass cut, highs down) layered over the current one for a phrase |
| Loop layering | Hawtin, Carl Cox, SHM | A bar of the other deck, high-passed above 450 Hz, looped under the mix for 8 bars |
| Dub delay throw | Hawtin's foot pedal | One 1/4-beat hit into the echo with feedback raised to 0.72 |
| Resonant filter sweep | Hawtin's Model 1 | Master low-pass at Q 9 down to 380 Hz and back |
| Sidechain pump | Alok | Master gain ducks to 20% on every beat and swells back |
| Pryda snare fill | Eric Prydz | Snare roll on the last beat before the bar |
| Crab / orbit scratch | DJ Qbert / DJ Disk | Four / two crossfader clicks per stroke |
| Mids-only reverb | Swedish House Mafia | Reverb send filtered to 250 Hz – 6 kHz |
| CO2 blast | Diplo, Aoki | Bright high-passed noise burst |
| Bass tease | Nucleya (dhol drop-out), Fisher | Bass cut for a bar, slammed back on the downbeat |
| Reverse, hard cut | Skrillex, DJ Snake, Kaytranada | Reverse the next beat; cut on the bar with no effect |

**Not built yet** (possible next steps): stem separation for true acapellas and drum-outs; a third and fourth deck;
key-shifting acapellas to match; a half-time/double-time switch; energy-arc scheduling for a whole set (Diplo:
pop → heavy → pop; Guetta's yo-yo); Kaytranada's swing; a vocal "mic hype" sample.
